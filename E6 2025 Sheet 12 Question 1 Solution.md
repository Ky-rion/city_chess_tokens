### 2. The Solution Explained: "Excitons" vs. "Continuum"

Your solution text says: _"Spectrum starts with two absorbance peaks and then transitions into continuum absorbance."_

Here is the physics translation of that sentence:

#### **The Continuum (The Slope on the Right)**

In a semiconductor, the most basic form of absorption is when a photon hits an electron in the **Valence Band** (where electrons are stuck) and kicks it all the way up to the **Conduction Band** (where they can move freely).

To do this, the photon needs at least as much energy as the **Band Gap ($E_g$)**.

- If Photon Energy $> E_g$: The electron is kicked free. This corresponds to the smooth line rising on the right side of your graph. We call this "Band-to-Band" absorption.
    

#### **The Peaks (The "Excitons")**

You might ask: _"Professor, if we need at least the Band Gap energy to kick an electron free, why are there absorption peaks **below** that energy (to the left of the slope)?"_

This is the cool part! Before an electron is completely kicked free, it can get "stuck" orbiting the hole it left behind. The electron is negative, the hole is positive, so they attract each other just like an electron orbits a proton in a Hydrogen atom.

We call this bound pair an **Exciton**.

- Because they attract each other, the system is slightly more stable (lower energy) than a free electron and a free hole.
    
- Therefore, you can create an exciton with slightly **less** energy than the full Band Gap.
    
- This is why those peaks appear _before_ (at lower energy than) the main slope.

### 3. Deciphering the Specific Peaks (Using the Solution)

The solution provides specific labels for those two peaks. Let's label your graph:

**Peak 1 (The Lower Energy Peak): The 1s Exciton**

- **The Solution says:** _"The lower-energy peak is the absorbance of an Exciton (1s state)."_
    
- **Physics Explanation:** Think of the Hydrogen atom. The lowest energy state is called the **1s** orbital. Similarly, the exciton has a "ground state" where the electron and hole are closest together. This is the most stable state, appearing at the lowest energy.
    

**Peak 2 (The Higher Energy Peak): The Mystery Peak**

- **The Solution says:** _"The next higher peak could be the 2s Peak... or the exciton consisting of a light hole and an electron."_
    
- **Option A (2s State):** Just like Hydrogen has excited states (2s, 2p, 3s...), an exciton can be excited. The electron orbits the hole further away. This requires a bit more energy than the 1s state, which is why this peak is to the right of the first one.
    
- **Option B (Light Hole):** In detailed semiconductor physics, there are actually two types of "holes" in the valence band: "Heavy Holes" and "Light Holes" (literally referring to their effective mass).
    
    - An exciton made with a **Heavy Hole** takes less energy (Peak 1).
        
    - An exciton made with a **Light Hole** takes more energy (Peak 2).
