### ⚡ Solid State Physics Cheat Sheet: Carbon Polymorphism

#### 1. The Comparison Table (High Yield)

| Feature | **Diamond** ($\diamond$) | **Graphite** ($\equiv$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Material** | Pure Carbon ($C$) | Pure Carbon ($C$) |
| **Hybridization** | **$sp^3$** | **$sp^2$** |
| **Mixing Recipe** | $1s + 3p$ orbitals mixed | $1s + 2p$ mixed ($1 p_z$ left over) |
| **Geometry** | **Tetrahedral** (3D Cage) | **Trigonal Planar** (Flat Sheets) |
| **Bond Angle** | **$109.5^\circ$** | **$120^\circ$** |
| **Bonding Type** | 4 strong $\sigma$-bonds (Covalent) | In-plane: 3 strong $\sigma$-bonds<br>Between planes: **Weak Van der Waals** |
| **Electrical** | **Insulator** (Band gap large) | **Conductor** (in-plane only) |
| **Mechanical** | Extremely **Hard** | **Soft** / Lubricant |
| **Symmetry** | **Isotropic** (Same in all directions) | **Anisotropic** (Direction matters) |

---

#### 2. Key Explanations (The "Why")

**Why is Diamond Hard & Insulating?**
*   **The Cage:** The $sp^3$ structure forms a rigid 3D network. There are no weak points to break.
*   **Locked Electrons:** All valence electrons are fixed in $\sigma$-bonds. No free electrons = No electricity.

**Why is Graphite Soft & Conductive?**
*   **The Sandwich:** The layers (Graphene) are strong, but the glue between them (Van der Waals) is weak. The layers slide easily (Soft).
*   **The $\pi$-Cloud:** The leftover $p_z$ orbitals stand up vertically and overlap. This creates a "sea" of **delocalized $\pi$-electrons**. These electrons can move freely along the sheet = Conductivity.

---

#### 3. Important Vocabulary

*   **Polymorphism:** Same atoms, different structure, different properties.
*   **Covalent Bond:** Sharing electrons (Strong). $\Delta EN = 0$ for Carbon-Carbon.
*   **Van der Waals Force:** Weak electrostatic attraction between neutral layers.
*   **Delocalization:** Electrons are not stuck to one atom; they roam freely (causes conductivity).
*   **Anisotropy:** Properties change depending on the direction (e.g., Graphite conducts *along* the sheet, but not *across* it).
