### 📝 Cheat Sheet: Bragg’s Law & X-Ray Diffraction

#### 1. The Holy Grail Equation

$$n \lambda = 2d \sin(\theta)$$

#### 2. Variable Definitions

-   **$n$ (Integer):** The "Order of Diffraction" ($1, 2, 3 \dots$). Usually, assume $n=1$ unless told otherwise.
    
-   **$\lambda$ (Lambda):** Wavelength of the incident X-ray beam.
    
-   **$d$:** Interplanar spacing (distance between crystal layers).
    
-   **$\theta$ (Theta):** The **Glancing Angle** (angle between the incoming beam and the **crystal surface**, NOT the normal!).
    

#### 3. Key Derivation Steps (The "Why")

If an exam asks you to "derive" or "show," these are the three relationships to list:

-   **Geometric Relation:**
    
    The extra distance one way is $g$.
    
    $$g = d \sin(\theta)$$
    
-   **Total Path Difference ($\Delta$):**
    
    The total extra distance traveled by the deeper wave (in + out).
    
    $$\Delta = 2g = 2d \sin(\theta)$$
    
-   **Constructive Interference Condition:**
    
    For a reflection to occur, the path difference must be a whole number of wavelengths.
    
    $$\Delta = n \lambda$$
    

#### 4. Exam Pitfalls & Tips ⚠️

-   **The Angle Trap:** If a problem says "the angle between the beam and the _normal_ is $30^\circ$," **DO NOT** plug $30$ into the formula. Bragg's law uses the angle from the _surface_. You must calculate $\theta = 90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ$.
    
-   **Units:** Make sure $\lambda$ and $d$ are in the same units (usually Angstroms $\mathring{A}$ or nanometers $nm$).
    
    -   $1 \mathring{A} = 0.1 \text{ nm} = 10^{-10} \text{ m}$
