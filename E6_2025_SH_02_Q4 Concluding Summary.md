# 🎓 Cheat Sheet: Electronic Structure & Bonding

### 1. Metallic Bonding Basics
*   **Model:** Positive ion lattice embedded in a "sea" of delocalized valence electrons.
*   **Strength Determinants:**
    1.  **Number of Valence Electrons (VE):** More electrons = "more glue."
    2.  **Ionic Charge:** Higher positive charge = stronger attraction to the electron sea.
*   **Coulomb’s Law (Qualitative):**
    $$F \propto \frac{q_{ion} \cdot q_{electron}}{r^2}$$
    *(Stronger charge $q$ $\rightarrow$ Stronger Force $\rightarrow$ Higher Melting Point)*

### 2. Group Comparisons (Melting Points)
*   **Alkali Metals (Group I):**
    *   Config: $ns^1$ ($\rightarrow$ +1 Ion, 1 VE).
    *   **Result:** Weakest metallic bond, soft, low MP.
*   **Alkaline Earth Metals (Group II):**
    *   Config: $ns^2$ ($\rightarrow$ +2 Ion, 2 VE).
    *   **Result:** Stronger bond, harder, higher MP than Group I.

### 3. The Group 12 Anomaly (Zn, Cd, Hg)
*   **Observation:** Low melting points compared to other transition metals.
*   **Configuration:** $(n-1)d^{10} ns^2$.
*   **Reason:** The **d-shell is full ($d^{10}$)**.
    *   Full shells are stable/inert.
    *   d-electrons **do not** participate in the metallic bond (unlike in Fe, W, Ti).
    *   Bonding relies only on the outer $s$-electrons (effectively behaving like Group II, but with heavier nuclei/weaker overlap).

---

### 4. Relativistic Effects (Why Mercury is Liquid)
*   **Condition:** Occurs in heavy atoms (High atomic number $Z$). Mercury $Z=80$.
*   **The Chain of Logic:**
    1.  **High Z:** Massive positive charge in nucleus.
    2.  **High Velocity:** Inner ($1s$) electrons move at $v \approx 0.86c$ to avoid collapsing into nucleus.
    3.  **Relativistic Mass Increase:** Mass increases near speed of light.
        $$m_{rel} = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{1 - (v/c)^2}}$$
    4.  **Orbital Contraction:** Bohr radius ($a_0$) is inversely proportional to mass.
        $$a_0 \propto \frac{1}{m_{e}}$$
        *If mass goes up, radius goes down.*
    5.  **Inert Pair Effect:** The outer **$6s$ orbital contracts** (stabilizes) and is pulled closer to the nucleus.
    6.  **Band Gap:** The energy gap between the filled Valence Band ($6s$) and empty Conduction Band ($6p$) increases.
*   **Result:** Valence electrons are "unavailable" for sharing $\rightarrow$ Extremely weak metallic bond $\rightarrow$ Liquid at Room Temp.

---

### 🔑 Keywords for the Exam
*   **Delocalized Electrons:** The "glue" of metallic bonding.
*   **$d^{10}$ Configuration:** Full d-shell = non-bonding d-electrons.
*   **Relativistic Contraction:** The shrinking of $s$-orbitals in heavy elements.
*   **Inert Pair Effect:** The tendency of the outermost $s^2$ pair to remain unshared in heavy post-transition metals.

Good luck with your studying! If you see a question about Mercury, just remember: **Heavy nucleus $\rightarrow$ Fast electrons $\rightarrow$ Heavy electrons $\rightarrow$ Tiny orbits $\rightarrow$ No sharing.**
