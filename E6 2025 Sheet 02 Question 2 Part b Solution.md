### Professor’s Explanation

Let's break down that German definition into plain English physics concepts. The solution describes the structure as a "recipe." To build this crystal, you need two ingredients: a **base lattice** and a specific way of **filling the holes**.

#### 1. The Base: The FCC Lattice ("Ein fcc-Gitter")
First, ignore the atoms floating *inside* the box for a moment. Look only at the **walls** of the cube in the diagram.
*   You see atoms at every **corner**.
*   You see atoms in the center of every **face**.

This arrangement is famous. It is called **Face-Centered Cubic (fcc)**. In German, it is often called *kubisch flächenzentriert (kfz)*. This is the standard structure for metals like Copper, Gold, and Aluminum.

#### 2. The Holes: Tetrahedral Voids ("Tetraederlücken")
Now, here is the tricky part. Even though the FCC atoms are packed tightly, there is still empty space between them. We call these empty spaces "voids" or "interstices."

In an FCC lattice, there are small holes located near each of the 8 corners.
*   Imagine standing in the very center of the box. Look toward one of the corners. Halfway between you and the corner atom, there is a "pocket" of empty space.
*   If you put a small marble in that pocket, it would touch exactly **4 atoms**:
    1.  The corner atom.
    2.  The three face-centered atoms touching that corner.

Because 4 atoms form a shape called a **Tetrahedron** (a pyramid with a triangular base), we call this hole a **Tetrahedral Void** (or *Tetraederlücke*).

Since a cube has 8 corners, an FCC lattice has **8 Tetrahedral Voids** inside every unit cell.

#### 3. The Filling: "With half occupied" ("mit der hälfte... besetzt")
Now, look at the diagram of the Diamond structure again.
*   We have 8 potential parking spots (voids) for interior atoms.
*   How many atoms are actually inside the box? **4**.

Since 4 is exactly half of 8, we say that **half of the tetrahedral voids are occupied**.

If you were to fill *all 8* voids, you would get a completely different structure called the **Fluorite structure** ($CaF_2$). But since we only fill *half* (usually in a zigzag pattern so they aren't right next to each other), we get the **Diamond Structure**.

### Summary for your notes

When you see this image in an exam, you can identify it by looking for two things:
1.  **The Shell:** Does it look like FCC (corners + faces)? **Yes.**
2.  **The Inside:** Does it have 4 atoms floating inside? **Yes.**

**Conclusion:** It is the **Diamond Cubic Structure**.

As I mentioned in part (a), this is the structure of Silicon and Germanium, the materials that make up the chips in your phone and computer. It is structurally very strong (which is why diamonds are hard!), but it has a lot of empty space (low packing density), which allows electrons to move around in interesting ways—making it perfect for semiconductors.
