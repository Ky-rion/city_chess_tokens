---

### 1. The Physics of the Continuous Spectrum

For a continuous spectrum to exist, the energy $E$ must be greater than the potential at infinity in at least one direction. As we discussed, that threshold is **$E > 2$**.

At this energy level:

- **On the Left ($x < -1$):** $E > V(x)$. The particle is classically allowed. It behaves like a traveling or standing wave.
    
- **In the Middle ($-1 \le x \le 0$):** $E$ is even further above $V(x)=0$. The particle has more kinetic energy here, so it moves "faster."
    
- **On the Right ($x > 0$):** The potential $V(x) = 2x^2$ eventually rises to meet $E$. There is still a classical turning point on the right side! No matter how high the energy $E$ is, that parabola will eventually get higher.
    

---

### 2. How to Draw the Sketch (The Rules of the Road)

When drawing a wavefunction at $E > 2$, you need to follow these three physical principles:

#### **A. The Wavelength Rule ($\lambda$)**

Kinetic energy is $E - V(x)$. The higher the kinetic energy, the shorter the wavelength (more "wiggles" per inch).

- **Interval A ($x < -1$):** Medium wavelength (since $E - 2$ is the kinetic energy).
    
- **Interval B ($-1 < x < 0$):** Shortest wavelength (since $E - 0$ is the largest kinetic energy). The wiggles should be more squished together here.
    
- **Interval C ($x > 0$):** The wavelength starts short but gets longer and longer as the potential $2x^2$ climbs toward $E$, slowing the particle down.
    

#### **B. The Amplitude Rule ($A$)**

The particle spends more time where it moves slowly. Therefore, the amplitude (height of the wiggles) is largest where kinetic energy is lowest.

- **Interval B:** Smallest amplitude (fast particle).
    
- **Interval A:** Larger amplitude than B.
    
- **Interval C:** Amplitude increases as you approach the turning point on the right.
    

#### **C. The Classical Turning Point & Tunneling**

Just like the ground state, there is a point on the right where $2x^2 = E$.

- **At the turning point:** The wiggles stop.
    
- **To the right of the turning point:** The wavefunction must transition into a decaying exponential tail (tunneling) that dies off toward zero, just like our $e^{-x^2}$ behavior from before.
    

---

### 3. Summary of the Drawing

If you were to draw this from left to right:

1. **Left ($x < -1$):** Draw a steady sine-wave with a constant amplitude and constant wavelength.
    
2. **Middle ($-1$ to $0$):** The wave suddenly gets "shorter" (smaller amplitude) and "faster" (more frequent wiggles).
    
3. **Right ($x > 0$):** The wave begins to stretch out. The peaks get taller and the gaps between peaks get wider as you move toward the right.
    
4. **The End:** At the point where the parabola $2x^2$ hits the energy $E$, the oscillations end with a final "hump" that decays smoothly into the right-hand wall.
    

**Crucial Note:** Unlike the ground state, this function **does not** go to zero on the left. It stays a constant oscillating wave forever toward $-\infty$ because there is no wall there to stop it!
