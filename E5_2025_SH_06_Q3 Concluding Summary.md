### 1. The Kinematic Variables

These are the building blocks for describing any scattering event.

-   **Center-of-Mass Energy Squared ($s$):** The total energy available in the "rest frame" of the collision.
    
-   **Momentum Transfer Squared ($Q^2$):** A measure of the "hardness" or resolution of the probe. Higher $Q^2$ means you are seeing smaller details.
    
-   **Bjorken $x$:** The fraction of the proton's momentum carried by the struck quark ($0 < x < 1$).

### 3. The "Double Angle" & Measurement Formulas

These are used when you want to calculate values based on what the detector actually sees (angles $\theta$ and $\gamma$).

-   **Scattered Electron Energy ($E'_e$):**
    
    $$E'_e = \frac{2E_e \sin(\gamma)}{\sin(\gamma) + \sin(\theta) + \sin(\gamma - \theta)}$$
    
-   **Momentum Transfer ($Q^2$) from Angles:**
    
    $$Q^2 = \frac{4E_e^2 \sin(\gamma) [1 - \cos(\theta)]}{[\sin(\gamma) + \sin(\theta) + \sin(\gamma - \theta)] c^2}$$
    
-   **Bjorken $x$ in Lab Frame:**
    
    $$x = \frac{E_e E'_e (1 - \cos \theta)}{2 E_p E_e - E_p E'_e (1 + \cos \theta)}$$
    

---

### 4. Key Concepts to Remember

-   **Efficiency:** Colliders are exponentially more efficient than fixed-target experiments for reaching high energies.
    
-   **Resolution:** Spatial resolution $\Delta x$ is inversely proportional to the momentum transfer: $\Delta x \approx \hbar / \sqrt{Q^2}$.
    
-   **Structure Functions ($F_2$):** Measuring $F_2$ across different $x$ and $Q^2$ values allows us to map out how quarks and gluons are distributed inside the proton.
    
-   **Detector Limits:** Your accessible physics "window" is always limited by your detector’s minimum energy threshold and its angular coverage.
