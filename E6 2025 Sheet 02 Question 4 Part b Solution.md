Welcome back to class! You are asking one of the most famous and fascinating questions in the periodic table.

Most people just accept that Mercury is a liquid, but as a physicist, you should find that deeply disturbing! It’s a metal! Metals are supposed to be hard solids. Why is Mercury (Hg) the rebellious teenager of the periodic table?

The solution provided by your other professor dives deep into **Quantum Mechanics** and **Relativity**. Yes, you heard that right—we need Einstein to explain why thermometers work.

Here is the breakdown of the solution for **Question 4b**.

***

### 1. The "Noble Gas" Disguise
First, let's look at Mercury's electron configuration. It ends in:
$$... 5d^{10} 6s^2$$

Just like Zinc and Cadmium (from Part A), Mercury has completely full sub-shells. The $d$-shell is full, and the $s$-shell is full.
*   **Chemistry 101 says:** Full shells are stable.
*   **The Problem:** Usually, metals overcome this stability by promoting electrons into a higher energy band (the "conduction band") so they can share electrons and bond.

However, Mercury holds onto its valence electrons ($6s^2$) remarkably tightly. It refuses to share them. Because the electrons aren't being shared effectively between atoms, the "metallic glue" we talked about in Part A is exceptionally weak.

**Weak glue = Low melting point.** (In this case, so low that it melts at -39°C!)

***

### 2. The Einstein Connection (Relativistic Effects)
This is the core of the professor's solution. He asks: *Why is Mercury so much "lazier" at bonding than Zinc or Cadmium?*

**A. The Speed of the Electron**
Mercury has a massive nucleus with **80 protons** ($Z=80$). This creates a gigantic positive charge.
The inner electrons (in the $1s$ shell) have to orbit furiously fast to avoid crashing into this massive nucleus. The solution notes state that these electrons move at **86% of the speed of light ($0.86c$)**.

**B. Relativistic Mass Increase**
When things move that fast, Einstein’s Special Relativity kicks in. As velocity approaches the speed of light, **mass increases**.
$$m_{rel} = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{1 - (v/c)^2}}$$
The mass of Mercury's electrons effectively increases because they are moving so fast.

**C. Orbital Contraction (The "Shrinkage")**
Here is a formula from the Bohr model of the atom regarding the radius of an orbit ($a_0$):
$$a_0 \propto \frac{1}{m}$$
Because the mass ($m$) of the electron has increased, the radius of the orbit **decreases**.
The electrons are pulled physically closer to the nucleus. This is called **Relativistic Contraction**.

***

### 3. The Consequences for Bonding
This contraction affects the $s$-orbitals the most (because $s$-electrons spend the most time near the nucleus).

**1. The "Inert Pair" Effect**
The outer $6s$ valence electrons are sucked inward toward the nucleus and stabilized (lowered in energy). They become "buried" deep inside the atom. They are so tightly held that they don't want to leave the atom to form a metallic bond. They act like the electrons in a Noble Gas (like Helium).

**2. The Band Gap Problem**
In a solid, we look at "Bands" rather than orbitals.
*   **Valence Band:** Full of electrons (from the $6s$).
*   **Conduction Band:** Empty space where electrons need to go to conduct electricity and form bonds (usually the $6p$ orbital).

In Zinc or Cadmium, these two bands overlap. It costs no energy to move an electron up and start bonding.
In Mercury, because the $6s$ orbital has been pulled down so low by relativity, a **gap** opens up between the filled $6s$ band and the empty $6p$ band.

The solution notes describe this perfectly: *"Das Leitungsband im Quecksilber ist komplett unbesetzt"* (The conduction band in Mercury is completely unoccupied).

***

### Summary: The Story of a Lonely Atom

1.  **Massive Nucleus:** Mercury has 80 protons.
2.  **Fast Electrons:** To survive the attraction, electrons zoom at 86% the speed of light.
3.  **Heavy Electrons:** Relativity makes these fast electrons gaining mass.
4.  **Tiny Orbits:** Heavier electrons orbit closer to the nucleus ($s$-orbital contraction).
5.  **Buried Valence:** The outer electrons ($6s^2$), which *should* be doing the bonding, are pulled tight against the atom.
6.  **No Bonding:** The atoms can barely grip each other. The metallic bond is so weak that mere room temperature heat is enough to break the solid apart.

**Result:** Mercury is a liquid.

*(Note: The solution also mentions that this large energy gap leads to "schlechte Leitfähigkeit" — poor conductivity. Mercury is indeed a much worse conductor of electricity than silver or copper, for exactly these reasons.)*
