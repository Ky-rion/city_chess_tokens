Excellent! Let’s move on to **Part b)**. This is a classic "visual identification" problem that appears in almost every solid-state physics exam.

The goal here is to look at the relationship between the **Energy Bands** (the black curves) and the **Fermi Energy** ($E_F$, the red dashed line) to classify the material.

**The Golden Rule:**

Think of the Fermi Energy ($E_F$) as the "sea level" for electrons at absolute zero temperature.

-   **Below $E_F$:** All states are filled with electrons (occupied).
    
-   **Above $E_F$:** All states are empty (unoccupied).
    

Let's break down each graph using your professor's solution images.

---

### a) Metal (Metall)

**Analysis:**

Look at graph **a**. Notice that the red dashed line ($E_F$) cuts right **through** the upper energy band.

-   The lower band is completely below the line (completely full).
    
-   The upper band is partly below and partly above the line (partially full).
    

**Why is it a Metal?**

Because the band is only partially full, there are empty energy states available just a tiny bit above the filled ones. This means electrons can easily gain a tiny bit of energy (from an electric field) and jump into a new state to move around.

-   **Analogy:** Imagine a half-full water bottle. If you tilt it, the water sloshes around easily. That "sloshing" is electrical current.
    

**The Sketch (Pink Dots):**

In the solution image **a**, you see pink dots filling the entire bottom curve and **filling the bottom half of the top curve**, stopping exactly at the red line.

---

### b) Semiconductor (Halbleiter)

**Analysis:**

Look at graph **b**. There is a clear gap between the lower curve and the upper curve. The Fermi Energy ($E_F$) sits comfortably in the middle of this "forbidden" gap.

-   The lower band (Valence Band) is completely full.
    
-   The upper band (Conduction Band) is completely empty.
    

**Why is it a Semiconductor?**

At absolute zero, this material cannot conduct electricity. To move, an electron would have to jump all the way across that big gap to find an empty seat.

-   **Analogy:** Imagine a water bottle that is filled 100% to the brim and capped. If you try to tilt it, the water cannot slosh because there is no empty space for it to move into.
    

**The Sketch (Pink Dots):**

In solution image **b**, the pink dots fill the **entire lower curve** up to the edge. The upper curve has **no dots** (it is empty).

---

### c) Semimetal (Halbmetall)

**Analysis:**

Look at graph **c**. This is the tricky one!

-   The top of the lower band (valence band) is actually _higher_ in energy than the bottom of the upper band (conduction band).
    
-   They **overlap** in energy, but at different locations in momentum space (different $k$ values).
    
-   The Fermi level cuts through both.
    

**Why is it a Semimetal?**

Because of this overlap, the electrons spill over. The "peak" of the valence band empties out (creating **holes**) and those electrons fall into the "valley" of the conduction band (creating **electrons**). This material conducts electricity, but typically with fewer charge carriers than a true metal.

**The Sketch (Pink Dots):**

In solution image **c**, look closely at the red line:

1.  **Upper Curve (Valley):** The bottom of this curve is below the red line, so it is filled with pink dots (these are the conduction electrons).
    
2.  **Lower Curve (Peak):** The very top of this curve is _above_ the red line, so it is **empty** (no dots—these are the holes). The sides of the curve are below the line, so they are filled.
