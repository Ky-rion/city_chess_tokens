Up until now, we have been looking at the crystal as a **Cube** (the unit cell).
Now, the question asks us to look at it as a stack of **Pancakes** (layers of spheres).

This is a classic solid-state physics question: "How do you stack oranges to make a cube?"

---

### The Question
**"Aus welcher Stapelreihenfolge einer hexagonal dichtesten Kugelpackung ergibt sich das Bravais Gitter/ Raumgitter der Kristallstruktur?"**
*(From which stacking sequence of a hexagonal closest packing does the Bravais lattice / space lattice of the crystal structure result?)*

### The Solution provided
**"Wie in Vorlesung gezeigt, bildet eine ABC Stapelung einer hcp-Schicht die fcc-Struktur."**
*(As shown in the lecture, an ABC stacking of an hcp-layer forms the fcc structure.)*

---

### Professor's Explanation

First, we must remember what we learned in part (b): The underlying **Bravais Lattice** of the Diamond structure is **FCC (Face-Centered Cubic)**.

So, this question is really asking: **"How do you stack layers of balls to build an FCC lattice?"**

#### 1. The First Layer (Layer A)
Imagine you are stacking oranges at a grocery store. You put the first layer down on the table. To pack them as tightly as possible, you arrange them in a **hexagon** pattern (one orange in the middle, six surrounding it).
*   We call this position **Layer A**.

#### 2. The Second Layer (Layer B)
Now you want to add a second layer. You don't put the new oranges directly on top of the old ones (that would be unstable). You place them in the little "dips" or "hollows" between the oranges of Layer A.
*   This new layer is in a different position. We call it **Layer B**.

#### 3. The Critical Moment: Layer C
Now you must place the third layer. You place the oranges in the hollows of Layer B.
Here, you have a choice, and this choice determines the entire crystal structure:

*   **Option 1 (The Hexagonal Way):** You place the third layer so the oranges are directly above the oranges in Layer A.
    *   Sequence: **A - B - A - B ...**
    *   Result: This creates **HCP (Hexagonal Close Packed)** structure. This is *not* what we want.

*   **Option 2 (The Cubic Way):** You look for the hollows in Layer B that are *not* directly above Layer A. There is a third unique position available.
    *   Sequence: **A - B - C - A - B - C ...**
    *   Result: This creates the **FCC (Face-Centered Cubic)** structure.

### Interpreting the Diagrams

Look at the solution images provided:

**The Left Image (The Stack):**
*   **Purple Balls:** This is Layer A.
*   **Green Balls:** This is Layer B.
*   **Blue Balls:** This is Layer C.
Notice how the Blue balls are not directly above the Purple ones? That is the **ABC** stacking.

**The Right Image (The Cube):**
This diagram is fascinating because it connects the "Stack" view to the "Cube" view.
*   If you take an FCC cube and balance it on its corner (so the body diagonal is pointing straight up), the layers of atoms form horizontal planes.
*   **Pink dots:** These correspond to Layer A (the corners).
*   **Teal dots:** These correspond to Layer B.
*   **Blue dots:** These correspond to Layer C.

### The Final Answer

The stacking sequence required to create the Bravais Lattice (FCC) for the Diamond structure is:

**A B C**

(or fully written out: **ABCABC...**)

*   If the professor asks for **Hexagonal Close Packed (hcp)**: The answer is **AB**.
*   If the professor asks for **Face Centered Cubic (fcc)** or **Diamond Lattice**: The answer is **ABC**.
