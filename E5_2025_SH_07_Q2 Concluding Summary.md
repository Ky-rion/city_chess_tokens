### **Part 1: The "Which Force is it?" Decision Tree**

When looking at a reaction, ask these questions in order to find the interaction type.

|**Check For...**|**If YES, then it is...**|**Notes / "Smoking Gun"**|
|---|---|---|
|**Neutrino ($\nu$)?**|**Weak**|The Weak force is the _only_ one that talks to neutrinos.|
|**Flavor Change?**|**Weak**|e.g., $s \to u$ (Strangeness changes). Look for $K \to \pi$ or $\Lambda \to p$.|
|**Photons ($\gamma$)?**|**Electromagnetic**|Usually a particle decaying into only photons (e.g., $\pi^0 \to \gamma\gamma$).|
|**Hadrons Only?**|**Strong**|If all particles are Baryons/Mesons and no flavor changes. Fast & "explosive."|

> **Pro Tip:** If you see a collision of charged leptons (like $e^+e^-$), it is primarily **Electromagnetic**, but at high energies, the **Weak** force ($Z$ boson) also plays a role.

### **Part 2: The "Particle Zoo" (Values for Accounting)**

You need to know the "bookkeeping values" for each particle to check conservation.

#### **A. Baryons (Heavy Weights)**

_Particles made of 3 quarks ($qqq$)._

- **Baryon Number ($B$) = +1** (Antimatter = -1)
    
- **Lepton Number ($L$) = 0**
    

|**Symbol**|**Particle**|**Charge (Q)**|**Mass (approx)**|
|---|---|---|---|
|$p$|Proton|$+1$|~938 MeV|
|$n$|Neutron|$0$|~940 MeV (Heavier than $p$!)|
|$\Lambda, \Sigma, \Xi$|Hyperons|$-1, 0, +1$|Heavy (contain Strange quarks)|

#### **B. Mesons (Middle Weights)**

_Particles made of a quark-antiquark pair ($q\bar{q}$)._

- **Baryon Number ($B$) = 0**
    
- **Lepton Number ($L$) = 0**
    

|**Symbol**|**Particle**|**Charge (Q)**|**Notes**|
|---|---|---|---|
|$\pi^+, \pi^-, \pi^0$|Pion|$+1, -1, 0$|The lightest hadrons.|
|$K^+, K^-, K^0$|Kaon|$+1, -1, 0$|"Strange" mesons (contain $s$ quark).|

#### **C. Leptons (Light Weights)**

_Point particles (no internal structure)._

- **Baryon Number ($B$) = 0**
    
- **Lepton Number ($L$) = +1** (Antimatter = -1)
    

|**Symbol**|**Particle**|**Charge (Q)**|**Partner Neutrino (L=+1)**|
|---|---|---|---|
|$e^-$|Electron|$-1$|$\nu_e$ (Electron Neutrino)|
|$\mu^-$|Muon|$-1$|$\nu_\mu$ (Muon Neutrino)|
|$\tau^-$|Tau|$-1$|$\nu_\tau$ (Tau Neutrino)|

### **Part 3: Conservation Laws Checklist**

For a reaction to be valid, **Left Side Total = Right Side Total**.

1. **Universal Conservation Laws** (Must ALWAYS hold true):
    
    - **Energy ($E$):** The mass of the _parent_ must be greater than the sum of the _children_ (for decays).
        
        - _Formula:_ $m_{parent} > \sum m_{products}$
            
    - **Electric Charge ($Q$):** Sum of charges must match.
        
    - **Baryon Number ($B$):** Total baryons must match.
        
    - **Lepton Number ($L$):** Total leptons must match.
        
    - **Momentum ($p$):** Linear and angular momentum.
        
2. **Conditional Conservation Laws** (Can be broken by the Weak Force):
    
    - **Strangeness ($S$):** Conserved in Strong/EM, but **violated** in Weak interactions.
        
    - **Parity ($P$):** "Mirror symmetry." Conserved in Strong/EM, **violated** in Weak interactions.

### **Part 4: Quick Examples for Exam Recall**

- **Beta Decay ($n \to p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$):**
    
    - _Force:_ Weak (see the neutrino?).
        
    - _Conserved:_ Charge ($0 \to 1 - 1 + 0$), Lepton No ($0 \to 0 + 1 - 1$), Baryon No ($1 \to 1 + 0 + 0$). All good!
        
- **Proton Decay ($p \to n + e^+ + \nu_e$):**
    
    - _Forbidden inside an atom?_ No, this happens in nuclei (Beta-plus decay) _if_ there is binding energy to spare.
        
    - _Forbidden in free space?_ **YES**. Because Mass($p$) < Mass($n$). You can't create mass out of nothing.
