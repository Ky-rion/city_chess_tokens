### Part 1: The Left Image – The Schottky Defect

Look at the diagram on the left. You see a nice checkerboard pattern of purple (large) and orange (small) spheres. This represents an **ionic crystal**, like table salt (NaCl).

-   **Purple Spheres:** Anions (negatively charged, usually larger).
    
-   **Orange Spheres:** Cations (positively charged, usually smaller).
    

Now, notice the dashed circles? Those are "holes" where atoms _should_ be, but aren't.

**The Professor's Note:**

> _"Links: Schottky-Defekte. Punktdefekte, Kation & Anion Fehlstelle (Ladungsneutralität), Dichte verringert sich gegenüber theoretischem Wert."_

**The Explanation:**

1.  **What happened?** In a Schottky defect, ions vacate their spots entirely. They essentially leave the crystal structure (migrating to the surface).
    
2.  **Charge Neutrality (Ladungsneutralität):** This is the most critical physics rule here. A crystal must stay electrically neutral. If a positive ion (cation) leaves, a negative ion (anion) _must_ also leave to balance the charge.
    
    -   That is why you see **two** missing spots in the diagram: one for a purple sphere and one for an orange sphere. They leave in pairs.
        
3.  **Density (Dichte):** The note says the "density decreases." Why?
    
    -   Density is Mass divided by Volume ($\rho = m/V$).
        
    -   The volume of the crystal doesn't change much (the lattice stays rigid).
        
    -   However, mass has physically left the building! Since mass goes down and volume stays the same, the overall density of the crystal drops.
        

**Analogy:** Imagine a classroom where every desk is full. If two students get up and walk out the door, the room is less "dense" with people. That is a Schottky defect.

### Part 2: The Right Image – The Frenkel Defect

Now, let's look at the diagram on the right.

**The Professor's Note:**

> _"Rechts: Frenkl-Defekt. Punktdefekt, Ion besetzt Zwischengitterplatz (meist Kation, da kleiner). Dichte bleibt unverändert."_
> 
> _(Note: The image has a small typo, usually spelled "Frenkel")_

**The Explanation:**

1.  **What happened?** Look closely at the center. An orange sphere (cation) has left its designated seat, but it didn't leave the crystal. Instead, it squeezed itself into a tiny gap between other atoms.
    
2.  **The Interstitial Site (Zwischengitterplatz):** The space between the regular rows of atoms is called an "interstitial site."
    
3.  **Why the Cation?** The note explains _"usually cation, as it is smaller"_. Imagine trying to squeeze a basketball (anion) into a small gap versus a tennis ball (cation). The smaller positive ion fits much easier into those tight spaces without breaking the crystal structure.
    
4.  **Density (Dichte):** The note says "density remains unchanged."
    
    -   The atom moved, but it is still _inside_ the crystal box.
        
    -   Total Mass: Same.
        
    -   Total Volume: Same.
        
    -   Therefore, the density stays exactly the same.
        

**Analogy:** Back to our classroom. A student gets up from their desk, but instead of leaving the room, they just go sit on the floor in the aisle between desks. The room is just as crowded (dense) as before; the person is just in a weird spot. That is a Frenkel defect.
