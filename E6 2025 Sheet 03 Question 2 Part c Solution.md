---

### 1. Drawing the (111) Plane

The question asks you to draw the **(111)** plane.

**The Logic:**

Using the Miller Indices $(hkl) = (111)$:

-   $x$-intercept at $1/1 = 1$
    
-   $y$-intercept at $1/1 = 1$
    
-   $z$-intercept at $1/1 = 1$
    

**The Drawing (Left image in solution):**

You simply draw a line connecting the unit vector on the x, y, and z axes. This forms a **triangle** that cuts off the corner of the cube.

**Which atoms are hit?**

This is the critical part. If you look closely at the blue triangle in the solution:

1.  It touches the **3 Corner Atoms** (at coordinates 1,0,0; 0,1,0; 0,0,1).
    
2.  It also slices perfectly through the **3 Face-Centered Atoms** located on the adjacent faces.
    
    -   _Why?_ Because these atoms form a tight triangle. In an FCC crystal, the atoms touch each other along the face diagonal.
        

---

### 2. The 2D Atomic Arrangement

The question asks for a sketch of the "two-dimensional atomic arrangement."

**Visualizing the Layer:**

Imagine you took that triangular slice out of the cube and laid it flat on a table. What would it look like?

-   The solution shows a group of green spheres packed tightly together (labeled "hcp" in the middle right).
    
-   Even though the crystal is a "cube," the atoms on this specific $(111)$ plane form a perfect **Hexagonal pattern**.
    
-   Each atom is surrounded by 6 other atoms.
    

**The Physics:**

This arrangement is called a **Close-Packed Layer**. It is impossible to pack spheres any closer together than this 2D hexagonal arrangement.

---

### 3. The Packing Scheme (ABC vs. AB)

The question asks: _"What packing scheme is this?"_

The solution text says: _"The atoms are hexagonal closest packed in the plane. The FCC structure results from a stacking of these planes in the **ABC sequence**."_

Here is the detailed translation of that concept:

**The Stacking Problem:**

If you have one layer of hexagonal balls (Layer A), and you want to put a second layer on top, you place the new balls in the "dimples" or gaps of the first layer (Layer B).

Now, where do you put the **third** layer? You have two choices:

1.  **The HCP Choice (ABAB...):**
    
    You place the third layer directly above the first layer. The pattern repeats: A-B-A-B.
    
    -   This creates a **Hexagonal Close Packed (HCP)** crystal (like Magnesium or Zinc).
        
2.  **The FCC Choice (ABCABC...):**
    
    You place the third layer in the _other_ set of gaps that line up with neither layer A nor layer B. This is a unique third position, "Layer C".
    
    -   The pattern is **A-B-C-A-B-C**.
        
    -   This creates a **Face-Centered Cubic (FCC)** crystal.
        

**Why is this mind-blowing?**

It means that **FCC and HCP are almost the same thing!** They are both built from the exact same hexagonal sheets of atoms. The _only_ difference is the order in which you stack the sheets.

-   If you look at FCC from the side (the cube face), it looks like squares.
    
-   If you look at FCC along the diagonal (the 111 direction), it looks like hexagonal layers.
