In part **a)**, we looked at the *result* (hard diamond vs. slippery graphite). In part **b)**, we are looking at the *cause*. We need to answer: **How exactly do the electron clouds (orbitals) rearrange themselves to make these shapes?**

This brings us to one of the most important concepts in solid-state physics and chemistry: **Hybridization**.

Think of hybridization like making a smoothie. You have different fruits (orbitals) with different shapes and energies. You throw them into a blender to create a new, uniform mixture that is perfect for bonding.

Let's break down the professor's solution image by image.

---

### 1. The Setup: Carbon's "Ground State"
Before Carbon bonds with anything, its electrons are arranged like this:
*   **1s:** Deep inside, full (2 electrons). We ignore these.
*   **2s:** A spherical cloud with 2 electrons.
*   **2p:** Dumbbell-shaped clouds with 2 electrons.

*Problem:* To bond, Carbon needs 4 "open slots" (unpaired electrons). But in its natural state, the $2s$ is full! So, Nature does a trick: it promotes an electron from the $2s$ to the empty $2p$ slot. Now we have 4 unpaired electrons ready to mingle.

Now, let's see how Diamond and Graphite mix these ingredients differently.

---

### 2. Diamond: The $sp^3$ Mixture (The "Tetrahedron")

Look at the top half of the solution image.

**The Recipe:**
*   Take **one** $s$-orbital.
*   Take **three** $p$-orbitals ($p_x, p_y, p_z$).
*   Mix them all together.
*   **Result:** You get **four** identical orbitals called **$sp^3$** (pronounced "s-p-three").

**The Physics (from the solution text):**
> *"4 Einfachbindungen über $sp^3$-Hybridorbitale"*
> *(4 single bonds via $sp^3$ hybrid orbitals)*

Because we mixed 4 ingredients, we get 4 new orbitals. They want to be as far away from each other as possible.
*   **Geometry:** The shape that separates 4 points equally in 3D space is a **Tetrahedron** (think of a camera tripod with a fourth leg sticking straight up).
*   **Bond Angle:** The magic number for a perfect tetrahedron is **$109.5^\circ$**.

**Why does this matter?**
This geometry is 3-dimensional and incredibly stable. Every electron is locked tightly in a strong "sigma" ($\sigma$) bond directly between atoms. This is why diamond is an insulator (no free electrons to carry electricity) and why it is so hard.

---

### 3. Graphite: The $sp^2$ Mixture (The "Flat Sheet")

Now, look at the bottom half of the solution image. This is the tricky part!

**The Recipe:**
*   Take **one** $s$-orbital.
*   Take only **two** $p$-orbitals ($p_x, p_y$).
*   **Leave one $p$-orbital ($p_z$) alone!** Do not put it in the blender.
*   **Result:** You get **three** identical **$sp^2$** orbitals and **one** leftover pure $p$-orbital.

**The Physics (from the solution text):**
> *"3 Einfachbindungen über $sp^2$-Hybridorbitale... trigonal planare Geometrie, Bindungswinkel $120^\circ$"*
> *(3 single bonds via $sp^2$ hybrid orbitals... trigonal planar geometry, bond angle $120^\circ$)*

**The Geometry (The Plane):**
The three $sp^2$ orbitals want to be far apart. The best way to separate 3 things is a flat triangle (like a Mercedes symbol).
*   This creates the flat honeycomb sheets.
*   The angle is **$120^\circ$**.

**The "Leftover" Electron (The $\pi$-Bond):**
> *"1 Doppelbindung über weiteres p-Orbital ($\pi$-Elektronen innerhalb Ebene delokalisiert...)"*

This is the coolest part of graphite. Remember that leftover $p$-orbital we didn't mix? It stands upright, perpendicular to the flat sheet (sticking up and down).
*   These vertical orbitals overlap sideways to form a **$\pi$-bond** (pi-bond).
*   The electrons in these $\pi$-bonds are not stuck in one place; they are **delocalized**. They form a "cloud" or "highway" above and below the sheet where electrons can zoom around freely.

**Why does this matter?**
Because these electrons are free to move through the "cloud" (the blue rings in the bottom image), **Graphite conducts electricity**! (But only along the sheets, not efficiently between them).
