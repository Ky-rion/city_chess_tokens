### 1. The Rule of Multiplicity: How many siblings?

In quantum mechanics, once you know the total spin (or Isospin) of a system, you automatically know how many variations of that system exist.

The rule is simple:

$$\text{Number of states} = 2I + 1$$

Since we calculated that the Isospin $I = 3/2$, we plug it in:

$$2 \left( \frac{3}{2} \right) + 1 = 3 + 1 = 4$$

**Conclusion:** There are **4 different $\Delta$ baryons**. This group is often called an "Isospin Quartet."

---

### 2. Identifying the Siblings ($I_3$)

Just like a ladder, the values for the "third component" ($I_3$) start at the top ($+I$) and go down in integer steps (subtracting 1 each time) until they hit the bottom ($-I$).

Let's walk down the ladder:

1. **Top rung:** $+3/2$
    
2. **Step down 1:** $+3/2 - 1 = +1/2$
    
3. **Step down 1:** $+1/2 - 1 = -1/2$
    
4. **Bottom rung:** $-1/2 - 1 = -3/2$
    

So, our four $I_3$ values are: **$3/2, 1/2, -1/2, -3/2$**.

---

### 3. Calculating the Electric Charge ($Q$)

Now we need to name them. In particle physics, we usually name particles by their electric charge (like the proton being positive).

We use the same **Gell-Mann–Nishijima relation** from Part (a), but this time we solve for Charge ($Q$):

$$Q = I_3 + \frac{Y}{2}$$

Recall from Part (a) that the Hypercharge $Y = 1$. So, for all $\Delta$ baryons, the formula simplifies to:

$$Q = I_3 + \frac{1}{2}$$

Let's calculate the charge for each of the four siblings, just as your solution image does:

#### Sibling 1: The One We Know ($I_3 = +3/2$)

$$Q = \frac{3}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{4}{2} = +2$$

- **Name:** $\Delta^{++}$ (Delta plus-plus)
    
- _This is the particle from Part (a)._
    

#### Sibling 2: The Next Step Down ($I_3 = +1/2$)

$$Q = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{2} = +1$$

- **Name:** $\Delta^{+}$ (Delta plus)
    
- _Note: This has the same charge as a proton!_
    

#### Sibling 3: The Neutral One ($I_3 = -1/2$)

$$Q = -\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 0$$

- **Name:** $\Delta^{0}$ (Delta zero)
    
- _Note: This has the same charge as a neutron!_
    

#### Sibling 4: The Negative One ($I_3 = -3/2$)

$$Q = -\frac{3}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = -\frac{2}{2} = -1$$

- **Name:** $\Delta^{-}$ (Delta minus)
    

---

### 4. The Final "Family Photo"

You asked to name them all. Here is the full quartet of $\Delta$ resonances found in nature. They are effectively the same particle, just "rotated" differently in isospin space:

1. **$\Delta^{++}$** (Charge +2)
    
2. **$\Delta^{+}$** (Charge +1)
    
3. **$\Delta^{0}$** (Charge 0)
    
4. **$\Delta^{-}$** (Charge -1)
    

This group of four sits at the very top of the famous "Baryon Decuplet" diagram (a triangle of particles), which was one of the key clues that led physicists to discover quarks.
