The question asks for the definition of a **crystal structure** and the components that make it up. The solution you provided gives us a wonderfully elegant and fundamental equation:

> **Lattice (Gitter) + Basis (Basis) = Crystal Structure (Kristallstruktur)**

To truly understand what this means, let's break down these two essential components.

---

### 1. The Lattice (Gitter)

Imagine a mathematically perfect, infinite grid of points in space. This is what we call a **Bravais lattice** (or simply the lattice).

-   **It is purely geometrical:** It's an abstract framework and does not contain any actual atoms.
    
-   **It defines the symmetry and repetition:** The lattice tells us _how_ a pattern repeats itself in three-dimensional space. If you were to stand on one lattice point and look around, the environment would look exactly the same as if you were standing on any other lattice point.
    

### 2. The Basis (Basis)

Now, we need to populate our mathematical grid with real matter. This is where the **basis** comes in.

-   **It is the physical building block:** The basis is the actual atom, molecule, or group of ions that sits at each lattice point.
    
-   **It defines the chemistry:** For a simple metal like copper, the basis is just a single copper atom. For a complex material like table salt (NaCl), the basis consists of one Sodium ion and one Chlorine ion. For organic crystals, the basis could be a complex protein molecule containing thousands of atoms!
    

### Putting it Together: The Crystal Structure

When you take the **basis** (the physical atoms) and attach it to every single point of the **lattice** (the geometric grid), you create the **crystal structure**.

Think of the lattice as the repeating blueprint of a wallpaper, and the basis as the specific flower or pattern that is printed at each spot on the blueprint. You need both the blueprint (lattice) and the stamp (basis) to create the final wallpaper (crystal structure).

---

### Why is this distinction important?

As we move forward in this course, you will see that different properties depend on different parts of this equation:

-   The **lattice** determines things like X-ray diffraction patterns and the shape of the Brillouin zones (which dictate electronic behavior).
    
-   The **basis** determines the actual density of the material, its optical properties, and its specific chemical reactivity.
