### 1. The Fundamental Equation
If you remember nothing else, remember this relationship. It is the definition of a crystal.

$$ \text{Crystal Structure} = \text{Lattice} + \text{Basis} $$

*   **Lattice (Gitter):** An infinite, periodic array of mathematical **points** in space. (The "Skeleton").
*   **Basis (Basis):** The group of **atoms** or molecules attached to every lattice point. (The "Flesh").
*   **Structure (Struktur):** The final physical result (Lattice $\times$ Basis).

---

### 2. Properties of the Components

**A. The Lattice (The "Map")**
*   **Periodicity:** The view from every lattice point must be **identical**.
*   **Bravais Lattice:** The specific name for these mathematical grids (e.g., Cubic, Hexagonal, Triclinic).
*   **Vector Definition:** Any point $\vec{R}$ in the lattice can be reached by integers ($n_1, n_2...$) and primitive vectors ($\vec{a}_1, \vec{a}_2...$):
    $$ \vec{R} = n_1 \vec{a}_1 + n_2 \vec{a}_2 + n_3 \vec{a}_3 $$

**B. The Basis (The "Stuff")**
*   **Composition:** Can be 1 atom (e.g., Copper), 2 atoms (e.g., Graphene/Salt), or thousands (Proteins).
*   **Orientation:** The Basis must have the **same orientation** at every lattice point. You cannot rotate it.
*   **Position:** The position of atoms in the basis is denoted by vectors $\vec{r}_j$ relative to the lattice point.

---

### 3. The Primitive Unit Cell (Primitive Gitterzelle)

**Definition:**
The smallest possible area (2D) or volume (3D) that can be stacked to fill all space without gaps or overlaps.

**The "Golden Rule" of Counting:**
A Primitive Unit Cell must contain exactly **1 Lattice Point**.

**How to Count Atoms in 2D (The "Corner Rule"):**
When you draw a box connecting lattice points:
*   **Corner points:** Count as $\frac{1}{4}$ (shared by 4 cells).
*   **Edge points:** Count as $\frac{1}{2}$ (shared by 2 cells).
*   **Inside points:** Count as $1$ (fully inside).

**Calculation for a standard primitive cell (parallelogram):**
$$ 4 \text{ corners} \times \frac{1}{4} = \mathbf{1 \text{ Lattice Point}} $$

---

### 4. Important Exam "Traps" (Watch out!)

**Trap 1: The "Atom is the Point" fallacy.**
*   *Wrong:* "Lattice points are always atoms."
*   *Right:* Lattice points are mathematical markers.
    *   If Basis = 1 atom $\rightarrow$ Points look like atoms.
    *   If Basis = Molecule $\rightarrow$ Points are often in the "empty" space between atoms (or defined arbitrarily on one specific atom of the molecule).

**Trap 2: The Honeycomb (Graphene) Lattice.**
*   *Visual:* It looks like a chicken wire (hexagons).
*   *Trap:* You cannot draw a Bravais lattice that hits *every* corner of a hexagon. The surroundings are not identical (one neighbor is up-left, another is down-right).
*   *Solution:* It is a **Triangular Lattice** (oblique) + a **2-Atom Basis**.
    *   Primitive Cell contains: 1 Lattice point = **2 Atoms** (1 Green + 1 Red).
