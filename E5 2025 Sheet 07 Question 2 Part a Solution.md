### The "Rules of Thumb" (Your Cheat Sheet)

Before we look at the specific answers, here is the secret logic particle physicists use to spot the right force immediately. Think of these as the "fingerprints" of the forces:

1. **The Strong Force:**
    
    - **The Players:** Involved _only_ with **Quarks** and particles made of quarks (called **Hadrons**, like Protons $p$, Neutrons $n$, Pions $\pi$, Deltas $\Delta$).
        
    - **The tell-tale sign:** If you see _only_ hadrons on both sides, and no "strangeness" is changing (we'll get to that), it is almost certainly the Strong force. It is super fast.
        
2. **The Electromagnetic Force (EM):**
    
    - **The Players:** Anything with **Electric Charge** or **Photons** ($\gamma$).
        
    - **The tell-tale sign:** Look for **Photons** ($\gamma$). If a particle decays purely into photons, it's EM.
        
3. **The Weak Force:**
    
    - **The Players:** It affects all matter particles (Quarks and Leptons).
        
    - **The tell-tale sign:** This is the only force that can change one type of particle into another (flavor change).
        
    - **The "Smoking Gun":** If you see a **Neutrino** ($\nu$) or an **Antineutrino** ($\bar{\nu}$), it is **always** the Weak interaction. Neutrinos do not feel the Strong or EM forces.

### Step-by-Step Solution Analysis

Now, let's look at the "solution matrix" provided by your other professor and explain why he put the "X"s where he did.

#### 1. Reaction: $K^+ \rightarrow \mu^+ + \nu_\mu$

- **What is happening:** A Kaon ($K^+$) is decaying into a Muon ($\mu^+$) and a Muon-Neutrino ($\nu_\mu$).
    
- **The Clues:**
    
    - The Kaon is a "Strange" particle (it contains a strange quark). The products (Muon) are not strange. We have changed the "flavor" of the quark.
        
    - **The Smoking Gun:** Do you see the $\nu_\mu$ (neutrino)?
        
- **The Verdict:** Because a neutrino is produced and quark flavor changed, this must be the **Weak Interaction**.
    
- **Matrix Answer:** **Weak**.
    

#### 2. Reaction: $\pi^0 \rightarrow \gamma + \gamma$

- **What is happening:** A neutral Pion ($\pi^0$) decays into two Photons ($\gamma$).
    
- **The Clues:**
    
    - The Pion is made of quarks, but it turns entirely into light (photons).
        
    - Photons are the carrier particles of the Electromagnetic force.
        
    - The Strong force holds quarks together, but it can't turn them into light. The Weak force is too slow for this.
        
- **The Verdict:** The decay into photons is the signature of the **Electromagnetic Interaction**.
    
- **Matrix Answer:** **Electromagnetic**.
    

#### 3. Reaction: $\Delta^+ \rightarrow n + \pi^+$

- **What is happening:** A Delta baryon ($\Delta^+$) falls apart into a Neutron ($n$) and a Pion ($\pi^+$).
    
- **The Clues:**
    
    - Every single particle here ($\Delta, n, \pi$) is a **Hadron** (made of quarks).
        
    - Does the "flavor" change? The $\Delta^+$ is made of `uud` quarks. The neutron is `udd` and pion is `u anti-d`. All the quarks are just rearranging themselves.
        
    - There are no neutrinos. No photons.
        
- **The Verdict:** This is a bunch of quarks rearranging themselves rapidly. This is the domain of the **Strong Interaction**.
    
- **Matrix Answer:** **Strong**.
    

#### 4. Reaction: $e^+ + e^- \rightarrow \mu^+ + \mu^-$

- **What is happening:** An electron ($e^-$) and its antimatter twin, the positron ($e^+$), crash into each other and create a muon pair.
    
- **The Clues:**
    
    - These particles have electric charge. When they crash, they usually annihilate into a virtual **Photon** ($\gamma$), which then turns into the muons. This makes it **Electromagnetic**.
        
    - _However, look at your professor's solution._ He marked **Weak** as well. Why?
        
    - **Professor's Insight:** In the Standard Model of physics, the Electromagnetic force and the Weak force are actually cousins (called the "Electroweak" force). While the photon does most of the work here, a heavy Weak particle called the **Z boson** ($Z^0$) can _also_ mediate this reaction. At low energies, we mostly see the EM part. At high energies, the Weak part becomes huge.
        
- **The Verdict:** It is primarily Electromagnetic, but technically mediated by both the photon and the $Z$ boson.
    
- **Matrix Answer:** **Electromagnetic AND Weak**.
    

#### 5. Reaction: $\bar{\nu}_\mu + e^- \rightarrow \bar{\nu}_\mu + e^-$

- **What is happening:** An antineutrino bounces off an electron (scattering).
    
- **The Clues:**
    
    - Look at the first particle: $\bar{\nu}_\mu$. It's a **Neutrino**.
        
    - Neutrinos have 0 electric charge (so they don't feel Magnetism/Electricity).
        
    - Neutrinos have 0 "color" charge (so they don't feel the Strong force).
        
    - They are "ghost" particles that only talk to the universe via the Weak force.
        
- **The Verdict:** Since a neutrino is involved, it has no other choice. It must be the **Weak Interaction**.
    
- **Matrix Answer:** **Weak**.
