#### 2. The High-Temperature Limit

_Use this when asked about the "Hochtemperaturgrenzwert" or why the models agree._

-   **The Law:** **Dulong-Petit Law**.
    
-   **The Value:** The Molar Heat Capacity approaches **$3R$** (approx. $24.94 \text{ J}/(\text{mol}\cdot\text{K})$).
    
-   **The Reason:**
    
    1.  Both models respect that a crystal with $N$ atoms has **$3N$ degrees of freedom**.
        
    2.  At high T, the **Equipartition Theorem** applies: every degree of freedom gets filled with $k_B T$ energy.
        
    3.  Therefore, the specific distribution of frequencies (density of states) stops mattering; only the total number of modes counts.
        

---

#### 3. Calculation Formulas

_Use these for numerical problems like the Gold example._

-   **Molar Heat Capacity ($C_{V,m}$):**
    
    $$C_{V,m} = 3 \cdot N_A \cdot k_B = 3R \approx 25 \text{ J}/(\text{mol}\cdot\text{K})$$
    
    _(Note: This approximation works for almost all simple elemental solids at room temp)._
    
-   **Heat Capacity for a specific mass ($C_V$):**
    
    $$C_V = n \cdot 3R$$
    
    where $n$ is the number of moles.
    
-   **Calculating Moles ($n$):**
    
    $$n = \frac{\text{Mass given in problem } (m)}{\text{Molar Mass from Periodic Table } (M)}$$
    
    -   **⚠️ Exam Trap:** Watch your units! If Molar Mass is in **g/mol**, make sure your sample mass is in **grams**.
        

---

#### 4. Key Constants

-   **$R$ (Gas Constant):** $\approx 8.314 \text{ J}/(\text{mol}\cdot\text{K})$
    
-   **$N_A$ (Avogadro's Number):** $\approx 6.022 \times 10^{23} \text{ mol}^{-1}$
    
-   **$k_B$ (Boltzmann Constant):** $\approx 1.38 \times 10^{-23} \text{ J/K}$
    
-   **Relation:** $R = N_A \cdot k_B$
